What This Equipment Does

The reason to specify a 10000bph water filling line carefully is that the cost of a poor fit is paid over years. This configuration is built for 330 ml can containers, 1213–1863 units per hour and a 1.5 ml tolerance, so the fit is decided before the order, not after commissioning.

Key Technical Specifications

Ask for the electrical single-line diagram and the duty cycle, not just the power total. This 10000bph water filling line draws 6.5–8.5 kW across a 3200 × 2200 × 2800 mm footprint with 316L stainless steel contact parts.

Capacity Planning and ROI

The business case for a 10000bph water filling line is arithmetic before it is technical: 1.5 ml per unit, 1213–1863 units per hour, and a shift count that adds up across a year. Work it that way and the answer is usually within 12–36 months at regional mid-size beverage producers scale.

Frequently Asked Questions

What accuracy can this 10000bph water filling line hold in real production?

The reference build holds ±1.5 ml on 330 ml can containers at 1213–1863 bottles per hour. In a real line the number to confirm is the deviation over a full shift rather than a short test.

How long does a format changeover take on this 10000bph water filling line?

With a standardised guide set the changeover is around 8 minutes. The first change on a new 330 ml can format takes longer because the guide set has to be set and taught.

Can the 10000bph water filling line run plastic and glass at the same time?

This configuration handles 330 ml can product on the specified format. Mixing materials usually means a cleaning and verification step between campaigns.

How do I size this for my plant?

Base capacity on peak hourly demand plus 15–20% headroom, and confirm utility availability before ordering.

Does it integrate with existing lines?

Yes, with standard PLC interfaces and shared CIP or control logic when specified at quotation.

What is typical lead time and service?

Standard machines ship in weeks; remote commissioning and spare parts support are usually included.
