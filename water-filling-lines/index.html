Water Filling Lines: 5-Gallon, Bottled and PET

A water filling line is mostly a water treatment project with a filling machine attached. The format of the container decides the filling principle, the capping system and most of the cost. This page sets out what each format needs.

What a water line actually contains

Almost every water line, from a 300 barrel-per-day workshop to a 20,000 bottles-per-hour plant, has the same five stages. What changes is the equipment inside each stage.

  1. Pre-treatment. Sand and carbon filtration, softening, then reverse osmosis for dissolved solids. UV and ozone are the usual disinfection steps downstream of the RO.
  2. Filling. The machine that puts water into the container. The principle depends on the container: gravity or pressure filling for bottles, a different head design for 5-gallon barrels.
  3. Capping. Screw caps for bottles, bung-type or snap caps for 5-gallon barrels. Cap feeding and torque control matter more than the capper itself.
  4. Labeling and coding. Sleeve, self-adhesive or roll-fed labels, plus a date/batch coder.
  5. Packing. Shrink wrap, carton erecting or bagging, then palletizing.
Practical rule: the treatment stage determines whether the product passes regulation; the filling stage determines line speed; the packing stage is where most unplanned downtime happens.

For 5-gallon lines the barrel washer is the real bottleneck, not the filler: a barrel that comes back from a customer needs interior brushing, caustic or disinfectant circulation, and a final rinse before it can be refilled. If barrel washing is undersized, the filler will sit idle regardless of its rated speed.

Water treatment is the quality gate

For bottled water the treatment train, not the filler, decides whether the product meets drinking-water standards. Three things are usually underestimated:

  • Feed water variability. RO membranes sized on a single lab report will underperform when the source changes seasonally. Size on the worst-case TDS and temperature, and keep a permeate conductivity meter on the line.
  • Ozone residual. Ozone is dosed to keep the filled water biologically stable, but too much leaves an off-taste and can oxidise components. Bromate formation is the regulatory risk where bromide is present in the source water.
  • Sanitary design after treatment. Water that is clean at the RO outlet can be recontaminated in a dead leg, an oversized buffer tank, or a poorly drained filler bowl.

Choosing a filler for your format

Work through these in order; each answer narrows the machine type.

  1. Container: barrel, bottle or large PET?
  2. Required output per shift, and how many shifts per day?
  3. Returnable or single-use containers? (Returnable adds a washer to the line.)
  4. Feed water source: municipal, well, or spring — and its TDS and iron content?
  5. Cap type: screw, sports cap, or barrel bung?
  6. Available floor space and ceiling height, including the treatment skid.
  7. Utilities: steam, compressed air, chilled water, and the electrical supply you actually have.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a 5-gallon water line and a bottled water line?

The container handling. A 5-gallon line fills 18.9 L returnable barrels and needs a barrel washer plus cap washing before refilling; a bottled water line fills small PET bottles, usually on a rinse-fill-cap monobloc, and needs no washer because the container is single-use.

Do I need reverse osmosis for bottled water?

It depends on the source. If the feed water has high dissolved solids, hardness or nitrate, RO is normally required to meet drinking-water standards; low-TDS municipal water may only need filtration and disinfection. The decision should follow a water analysis of your actual source.

How do I choose between gravity and pressure filling?

Pressure filling is used where the container or product needs it — carbonated products and rigid large containers. Still water in small PET bottles is usually gravity filled, which is simpler and gentler on the bottle.

Can one line handle both 5-gallon barrels and bottles?

Not practically. The filler heads, capping system and container handling are different, and the barrel line additionally needs a washer. Running two formats usually means two lines, or a single line with a changeover that costs more downtime than it saves.
